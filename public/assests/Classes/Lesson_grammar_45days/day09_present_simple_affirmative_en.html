🗺️ GRAMMAR MAP: Present Simple: Affirmative & Spelling Rules
1. Base verb: I / you / we / they
play → play
I play chess after school.
💡 The subject “I” takes the base form play, not plays.
read → read
They read comics every evening.
💡 “They” is plural, so the verb remains read.
2. Third-person singular: -s / -es
watch → watches
My brother watches cartoons on Sunday.
💡 Watch ends in -ch, so -es makes watches.
go → goes
Dad goes to work by bus.
💡 Go ends in -o and uses the common -es form goes.
3. Spelling changes and exceptions
have + she → has
She has a red pencil case.
💡 The correct form is has, not haves: have is irregular in the third-person singular.
have + they → have
They have new notebooks.
💡 “They” takes the base form have; has is only for third-person singular subjects.

📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. I / you / we / they: use the base verb

The present simple expresses habits and repeated actions. With these subjects, the lexical verb stays in its base form.

play → play
I play chess after school.
💡 The subject “I” takes the base form play, not plays.
read → read
They read comics every evening.
💡 “They” is plural, so the verb remains read.

2. He / she / it: add -s

In affirmative present-simple statements, most verbs take -s with a third-person singular subject.

play + she → plays
She plays chess on Fridays.
💡 “She” is third-person singular; play normally takes -s.
read + he → reads
He reads a story before bed.
💡 “He” requires the third-person singular ending -s.

3. Endings -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -z, -o: usually add -es

Some endings take -es in the third-person singular: watch → watches; go → goes.

watch → watches
My brother watches cartoons on Sunday.
💡 Watch ends in -ch, so -es makes watches.
go → goes
Dad goes to work by bus.
💡 Go ends in -o and uses the common -es form goes.
wash → washes
Mina washes her hands before lunch.
💡 Wash ends in -sh, so add -es.
fix → fixes
The mechanic fixes bicycles.
💡 Fix ends in -x, so add -es rather than only -s.

4. Consonant + y: change y to -ies

When a consonant comes immediately before final -y, replace y with -ies.

study → studies
Linh studies English every day.
💡 In study, d is a consonant before y, so study becomes studies.
carry → carries
The boy carries his schoolbag.
💡 In carry, r is a consonant before y, so the ending becomes -ies.

5. Vowel + y: keep y and add -s

When a vowel immediately precedes -y, keep y and add only -s.

play → plays
Anna plays the piano after school.
💡 The letter a before y is a vowel; keep y and add -s.
enjoy → enjoys
My cat enjoys warm sunshine.
💡 The letter o before y is a vowel; enjoy becomes enjoys, not enjoies.

6. Irregular forms: have → has; be → am/is/are

Have has the special third-person singular form has. The verb be uses am, is, or are according to the subject.

have + she → has
She has a red pencil case.
💡 The correct form is has, not haves: have is irregular in the third-person singular.
have + they → have
They have new notebooks.
💡 “They” takes the base form have; has is only for third-person singular subjects.
be + I → am
I am ready for class.
💡 Be changes to am with I instead of taking an -s ending.
be + she → is
She is my best friend.
💡 Be changes to is with she; “bes” is never used.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She play tennis.
✅ She plays tennis.
💡 She is third-person singular, so play needs -s.
MISTAKE 2
❌ He studys every night.
✅ He studies every night.
💡 Study ends in consonant + y; change y to -ies.
MISTAKE 3
❌ My sister haves a bike.
✅ My sister has a bike.
💡 Have changes irregularly to has with a singular third-person subject.
MISTAKE 4
❌ They goes to school.
✅ They go to school.
💡 They takes the base verb go, not goes.

❓ Quick FAQ

When do we use the present simple?

For habits, routines, and general facts, such as “I walk to school every day.”

Does every verb add -s with he / she / it?

No. Some take -es or -ies; have becomes has and be becomes is.

Do these rules apply after does?

No. After does or does not, use the base verb: “Does she play?”
