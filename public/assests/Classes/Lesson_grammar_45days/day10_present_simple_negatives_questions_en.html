🗺️ GRAMMAR MAP: Present Simple: Negatives & Yes/No Questions
1. Do / does + not + base verb
play → do not play
I do not play games at night.
💡 I takes do not; the main verb stays play.
eat → don’t eat
They don’t eat candy before dinner.
💡 They takes don’t and the verb remains eat, not eats.
2. Do / Does + subject + base verb?
you play → Do you play?
Do you play badminton after class?
💡 The auxiliary do begins the question; play remains in its base form.
they live → Do they live?
Do they live near the school?
💡 They pairs with do, not does.
3. Short answers and be exceptions
she is → she is not
She is not tired.
💡 The verb is makes its own negative; “doesn’t is” is incorrect.
they are → Are they...?
Are they ready for the test?
💡 Move are before they; do is unnecessary.

📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. Negative with I / you / we / they: do not / don’t

Put do not (don’t) before the base form of an ordinary verb.

play → do not play
I do not play games at night.
💡 I takes do not; the main verb stays play.
eat → don’t eat
They don’t eat candy before dinner.
💡 They takes don’t and the verb remains eat, not eats.

2. Negative with he / she / it: does not / doesn’t

Use does not (doesn’t) + base verb. Do not attach -s or -es to the main verb.

likes → does not like
She does not like spicy food.
💡 Does already marks third-person singular, so like has no -s.
watches → doesn’t watch
He doesn’t watch TV before school.
💡 With doesn’t, watch returns to its base form instead of watches.

3. Yes/No questions with do

Move do to the front when the subject is I, you, we, or they.

you play → Do you play?
Do you play badminton after class?
💡 The auxiliary do begins the question; play remains in its base form.
they live → Do they live?
Do they live near the school?
💡 They pairs with do, not does.

4. Yes/No questions with does

Use does + he/she/it + base verb. The -s appears on does, not on the main verb.

she studies → Does she study?
Does she study English every day?
💡 Does carries the singular marker; study is not studies after does.
he goes → Does he go?
Does he go to school by bus?
💡 After does, goes changes back to go.

5. Short answers: Yes/No + subject + do/does

Repeat the auxiliary in the short answer; do not repeat the main verb.

Do you swim? → Yes, I do.
Do you swim? — Yes, I do.
💡 The question uses do, so the short affirmative answer is “I do.”
Does Mia sing? → No, she doesn’t.
Does Mia sing? — No, she doesn’t.
💡 Mia is she, a third-person singular subject, so the answer uses doesn’t.

6. Be questions and negatives: no do/does

For am/is/are as the main verb, add not for negatives or place be before the subject for questions.

she is → she is not
She is not tired.
💡 The verb is makes its own negative; “doesn’t is” is incorrect.
they are → Are they...?
Are they ready for the test?
💡 Move are before they; do is unnecessary.
I am → I am not
I am not late today.
💡 With I, be takes am; its negative is am not.
he is → Is he...?
Is he your classmate?
💡 A be question begins with is, not does.

🎯 2. Real-World Usage Scenarios

Planning a game

Do you play basketball? — Yes, I do.
My sister doesn’t play basketball, but she plays tennis.

In the classroom

Does your teacher give homework on Friday?
No, she doesn’t.

At home

We don’t watch TV during dinner.
Is your brother at home?

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She doesn’t likes milk.
✅ She doesn’t like milk.
💡 Doesn’t already carries the third-person ending; like stays in base form.
MISTAKE 2
❌ Does he goes to school?
✅ Does he go to school?
💡 After does, always use go, not goes.
MISTAKE 3
❌ Do she read books?
✅ Does she read books?
💡 She requires does, not do.
MISTAKE 4
❌ Does she is happy?
✅ Is she happy?
💡 With be, invert is and the subject; do/does is not used.

❓ Quick FAQ

Do “don’t” and “doesn’t” mean the same thing?

Both make a negative, but don’t goes with I/you/we/they and doesn’t with he/she/it.

Why is there no -s after does?

Because does already marks third-person singular; the main verb must remain in base form.

Can I use do with the verb be?

Not in ordinary be negatives and questions: say “Is she ready?”, not “Does she is ready?”
