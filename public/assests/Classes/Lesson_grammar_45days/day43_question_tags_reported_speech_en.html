📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

Positive statement → negative tag

The tag normally switches polarity and repeats the correct auxiliary.

is → isn’t it?
It is sunny, isn’t it?
💡 Positive is takes a negative is-tag; it repeats the subject.
can → can’t she?
She can swim, can’t she?
💡 A positive can clause takes a negative can tag.

Negative statement → positive tag

Negative clauses typically take positive tags.

isn’t → is he?
He isn’t late, is he?
💡 The negative isn’t is balanced by positive is.
don’t → do they?
They don’t eat fish, do they?
💡 Don’t supplies do for the positive tag.

Present reporting: say/tell

Tell usually needs an object; say need not take one.

say → says that
Mia says that she likes art.
💡 Says can introduce a that-clause directly.
tell → tells me that
Tom tells me that he is ready.
💡 Tell is followed by the listener me.

Past reporting: tense backshift

When reporting from a past viewpoint, present often shifts to past.

am → was
She said that she was tired.
💡 Said sets a past viewpoint, so am backshifts to was.
will → would
He said that he would help us.
💡 Will commonly backshifts to would after past said.

Pronoun/time adjustments

Adjust pronouns and time expressions according to the reporting context.

I → she
Anna said that she was ready.
💡 The original speaker I becomes she when Anna is reported.
tomorrow → the next day
He said he would come the next day.
💡 From a past reporting point, tomorrow may become the next day.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ It is sunny, is it?
✅ It is sunny, isn’t it?
💡 Positive is takes a negative is-tag; it repeats the subject.
MISTAKE 2
❌ He isn’t late, isn’t he?
✅ He isn’t late, is he?
💡 The negative isn’t is balanced by positive is.
MISTAKE 3
❌ Tom told that he was ready.
✅ Tom said that he was ready.
💡 Says can introduce a that-clause directly.
MISTAKE 4
❌ She said that she is tired. (past reporting context)
✅ She said that she was tired.
💡 Said sets a past viewpoint, so am backshifts to was.
