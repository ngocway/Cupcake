📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

Who for people

Who introduces a defining clause about a person.

person + who
The girl who won is my friend.
💡 Who refers back to the girl, a person.
teacher + who
The teacher who helps us is kind.
💡 Who identifies which teacher we mean.

Which for things and animals

Which introduces a relative clause about a thing or animal.

book + which
The book which I bought is funny.
💡 Which refers to book, not a person.
dog + which
The dog which barked is ours.
💡 Which relates to the animal dog.

That for people or things

That may replace who or which in many defining clauses.

boy + that
The boy that called is Tom.
💡 That refers to a person in a defining clause.
cake + that
The cake that Mum made is delicious.
💡 That refers to the cake as the object of made.

Whose indicates possession

Whose connects a person or thing to something they possess.

girl + whose bag
The girl whose bag is red is Anna.
💡 Whose links the bag to the girl as its owner.
student + whose dad
The student whose dad is a doctor sits here.
💡 Whose shows a family relationship, not which.

Omit an object relative pronoun

In defining clauses, an object who/which/that can often be omitted.

that I read → I read
The story I read was exciting.
💡 The relative word is an object of read, so omission is possible.
that we met → we met
The boy we met is Ben.
💡 The omitted relative word is the object of met.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ The girl which won is my friend.
✅ The girl who won is my friend.
💡 Who refers back to the girl, a person.
MISTAKE 2
❌ The book who I bought is funny.
✅ The book which I bought is funny.
💡 Which refers to book, not a person.
MISTAKE 3
❌ The cake whose I ate was nice.
✅ The cake that I ate was nice.
💡 That refers to a person in a defining clause.
MISTAKE 4
❌ The boy who bike is red is Tom.
✅ The boy whose bike is red is Tom.
💡 Whose links the bag to the girl as its owner.

❓ Quick FAQ

Which form should I choose?

Who introduces a defining clause about a person.

What is an important exception?

In defining clauses, an object who/which/that can often be omitted.
