🗺️ GRAMMAR MAP: Adverbs of Frequency (Always, Usually, Often...)
1. Frequency scale: always → never
every time → always
I always brush my teeth before bed.
💡 Always signals that the action happens on every relevant occasion.
not at any time → never
We never leave litter in the park.
💡 Never means zero occasions and already has negative meaning.
2. Position before main verbs
eat + always → always eat
They always eat breakfast at home.
💡 Always precedes the main verb eat.
often + reads → often reads
Mai often reads after dinner.
💡 Even when reads takes -s, often stays before the verb.
3. Position after be; questions and negatives
sometimes + we play
Sometimes, we play games after school.
💡 A beginning position is natural for sometimes, separated by a comma.
we play + sometimes
We play games after school sometimes.
💡 Sometimes can also go at the end without changing the basic meaning.

📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. Understand frequency words

These adverbs describe how regularly an action happens; percentages are only rough learning guides, not fixed rules.

every time → always
I always brush my teeth before bed.
💡 Always signals that the action happens on every relevant occasion.
not at any time → never
We never leave litter in the park.
💡 Never means zero occasions and already has negative meaning.

2. Usually / often / sometimes: different degrees

Usually means in most cases, often means frequently, and sometimes means on some occasions.

most days → usually
She usually walks to school.
💡 Usually is appropriate when walking is her normal routine, with exceptions.
many occasions → often
We often play chess at lunch.
💡 Often tells us the activity happens frequently, but not necessarily every day.
some occasions → sometimes
My brother sometimes makes pancakes.
💡 Sometimes indicates occasional, not habitual, repetition.
almost no occasions → rarely
I rarely drink soda.
💡 Rarely signals low frequency, though not necessarily zero.

3. Before ordinary action verbs

Place most mid-position frequency adverbs before the main action verb.

eat + always → always eat
They always eat breakfast at home.
💡 Always precedes the main verb eat.
often + reads → often reads
Mai often reads after dinner.
💡 Even when reads takes -s, often stays before the verb.

4. After am / is / are

With be as the main verb, put the frequency adverb after am, is, or are.

is + usually → is usually
My sister is usually cheerful.
💡 Usually follows is rather than appearing before it.
are + never → are never
The students are never late.
💡 Never goes after are in this be sentence.

5. Questions, negatives, and auxiliary verbs

In ordinary questions, the adverb usually follows the subject; with auxiliaries, it usually comes after the first auxiliary.

Do + you + often + play?
Do you often play outside?
💡 Often follows the subject you and precedes play in the question.
doesn’t + usually + eat
He doesn’t usually eat snacks at night.
💡 Usually comes after doesn’t and before the base verb eat.

6. Sometimes: flexible position; never: no extra not

Sometimes can appear at the start or end of a sentence. Never already makes the meaning negative.

sometimes + we play
Sometimes, we play games after school.
💡 A beginning position is natural for sometimes, separated by a comma.
we play + sometimes
We play games after school sometimes.
💡 Sometimes can also go at the end without changing the basic meaning.
never + eat
I never eat candy before breakfast.
💡 Never already expresses “not ever”, so no don’t is needed.
never + forgets
She never forgets her homework.
💡 The verb stays affirmative in form after never; never supplies the negative meaning.

🎯 2. Real-World Usage Scenarios

Morning routine

I usually get up at seven.
My brother sometimes sleeps late.

School life

Our teacher is always kind.
Do you often read in the library?

Healthy habits

We never skip breakfast.
Dad rarely drinks soda.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She goes often to school by bus.
✅ She often goes to school by bus.
💡 Often normally goes before the main verb goes.
MISTAKE 2
❌ My dad always is busy.
✅ My dad is always busy.
💡 With be, always comes after is.
MISTAKE 3
❌ I don’t never eat fruit.
✅ I never eat fruit.
💡 Never already carries negative meaning; don’t + never makes an unintended double negative in standard usage.
MISTAKE 4
❌ Does she plays often tennis?
✅ Does she often play tennis?
💡 In the question, often precedes the base verb play; does requires play, not plays.

❓ Quick FAQ

Is “always” exactly 100%?

It generally suggests every relevant occasion; percentages in charts are approximate teaching aids.

Can “sometimes” start a sentence?

Yes: “Sometimes, I ride my bike.”

Is “never” a negative word?

Yes. Say “She never eats fish,” not “She doesn’t never eat fish” in standard English.
