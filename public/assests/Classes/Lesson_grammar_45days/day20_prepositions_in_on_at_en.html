📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. In + months, years and broad periods

Use in with months, years, seasons and broad parts of the day.

July → in July
My birthday is in July.
💡 July names a month, so use in.
2026 → in 2026
We start our club in 2026.
💡 A year is a broad time period, so in is used.
the morning → in the morning
I do exercises in the morning.
💡 Morning is a broad part of the day, so in is appropriate.

2. On + days and dates

Use on before weekdays and calendar dates.

Monday → on Monday
We have art on Monday.
💡 Monday is a named day, so it takes on.
May 5 → on May 5
The concert is on May 5.
💡 May 5 identifies a specific date, so use on.

3. At + exact times and fixed points

Use at with clock times and fixed expressions such as at noon and at night.

7:30 → at 7:30
School begins at 7:30.
💡 7:30 is an exact clock time, so it takes at.
noon → at noon
We eat lunch at noon.
💡 Noon is a fixed time point, so use at.
night → at night
The stars shine at night.
💡 At night is a standard fixed expression, unlike in the morning.

4. On + a specific day morning / afternoon / evening

When a part of the day is tied to a named day/date, use on instead of in.

Monday + morning → on Monday morning
We practise on Monday morning.
💡 Monday makes the morning specific, so on replaces in.
Friday + evening → on Friday evening
Our family visits Grandma on Friday evening.
💡 Friday specifies the evening, so the phrase takes on.

5. In + enclosed areas and larger places

Use in to say someone or something is inside an area, room, city or country.

classroom → in the classroom
The students are in the classroom.
💡 The classroom is an enclosed space, so use in.
Hanoi → in Hanoi
My aunt lives in Hanoi.
💡 Hanoi is a city viewed as a larger area, so use in.

6. On + surfaces

Use on when something touches or is supported by a surface.

desk → on the desk
The pencil is on the desk.
💡 The desk supports the pencil on its surface.
wall → on the wall
There is a map on the wall.
💡 The map is attached to the wall surface.

7. At + specific points and meeting places

Use at for a particular point, entrance, stop or meeting location.

bus stop → at the bus stop
We meet at the bus stop.
💡 The bus stop is treated as a specific meeting point.
door → at the door
Dad is waiting at the door.
💡 The door is a particular point where someone waits.

8. No preposition before every, next, last, this

Time expressions beginning with every, next, last or this usually take no in/on/at.

on + next Monday → next Monday
We have a test next Monday.
💡 Next already specifies the day; do not add on.
in + this morning → this morning
I met my friend this morning.
💡 This already specifies the time expression, so in is omitted.

🎯 2. Real-World Usage Scenarios

Planning a class trip

Our trip is on Friday.
We meet at 8:00 at the school gate.

At home

The keys are on the table.
My brother is studying in his room.

Talking about holidays

Our family travels in December.
We usually eat together at noon.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ We meet in Monday.
✅ We meet on Monday.
💡 Days of the week take on, not in.
MISTAKE 2
❌ I wake up on 6:30.
✅ I wake up at 6:30.
💡 An exact clock time takes at.
MISTAKE 3
❌ The book is in the table.
✅ The book is on the table.
💡 The book rests on the table surface, so use on.
MISTAKE 4
❌ I will see you on next Friday.
✅ I will see you next Friday.
💡 Next Friday normally has no preceding preposition.

❓ Quick FAQ

Why “at night” but “in the morning”?

They are established time expressions: at night, but in the morning/afternoon/evening.

Do we say “on Monday morning”?

Yes. A named day makes the morning specific, so use on.

Is it “at school” or “in school”?

Both exist: at school commonly means at the school location; in school often means enrolled/attending school.
