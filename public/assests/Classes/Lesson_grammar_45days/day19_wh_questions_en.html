📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. What: ask about things and activities

Use what when asking about information, objects, or activities.

you do → What do you do?
What do you do after school?
💡 What requests the activity; do helps form a present simple question.
she likes → What does she like?
What does she like to read?
💡 Does agrees with she; like stays in its base form after does.

2. Where: ask about location

Put where at the beginning of a location question.

you live → Where do you live?
Where do you live?
💡 Where seeks a place; do is used with you in the present simple.
the library is → Where is the library?
Where is the library?
💡 With be, move is before the subject; do is not needed.

3. When: ask about time

Use when for days, dates, or times of an event.

class starts → When does class start?
When does class start?
💡 Class is singular, so does is used and start remains base form.
they play → When do they play?
When do they play football?
💡 They takes do in a present simple WH-question.

4. Who as subject: no do/does needed

When who itself is the subject of a simple-present question, normally do not insert do or does.

someone teaches → Who teaches?
Who teaches your class?
💡 Who is the subject performing teaches, so no does is inserted.
someone lives here → Who lives here?
Who lives next door?
💡 Who replaces the unknown subject; lives carries third-person singular -s.

5. Who as object: use do/does

When asking about the person receiving an action, retain the subject and use do/does.

you call someone → Who do you call?
Who do you call after class?
💡 You is the subject doing the calling; who asks for the object.
she helps someone → Who does she help?
Who does she help on Saturdays?
💡 She is the subject; does appears and help stays in base form.

6. Why: ask for a reason

Use why to ask for an explanation, often answered with because.

you smile → Why do you smile?
Why do you smile? — Because I am happy.
💡 Why asks the reason, not a place or time.
she is tired → Why is she tired?
Why is she tired? — Because she works hard.
💡 With be, invert is and she; no does is required.

7. How / How often: ask about manner and frequency

How asks about manner or condition; how often asks about frequency.

you go → How do you go?
How do you go to school? — By bus.
💡 How asks about the method of travel.
you read → How often do you read?
How often do you read books? — Every evening.
💡 How often asks how frequently an action happens.

8. WH-questions in the present continuous

Use WH-word + am/is/are + subject + verb-ing to ask about an ongoing action.

you are drawing → What are you drawing?
What are you drawing now?
💡 Are moves before you; drawing keeps -ing because the action is underway.
she is going → Where is she going?
Where is she going?
💡 Where asks for her destination; is + going marks present continuous.

🎯 2. Real-World Usage Scenarios

Asking a new classmate

Where do you live? — Near the park.
What do you do after school? — I play football.

In the library

Who teaches English here? — Ms. Lan.
When does the library close? — At five.

On the playground

What are you playing? — Tag.
Why are you laughing? — Because the game is funny.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ Where you live?
✅ Where do you live?
💡 A present-simple question with lexical live needs do.
MISTAKE 2
❌ What does she likes?
✅ What does she like?
💡 After does, the main verb stays in base form.
MISTAKE 3
❌ Who does teach your class?
✅ Who teaches your class?
💡 Who is the subject; do/does is not normally inserted.
MISTAKE 4
❌ What you are drawing?
✅ What are you drawing?
💡 In a continuous WH-question, are precedes the subject you.

❓ Quick FAQ

Do all WH-questions need do or does?

No. Questions with be and subject questions such as “Who teaches?” do not.

What is the difference between when and how often?

When asks for a time; how often asks for frequency.

Can who be both a subject and an object?

Yes: “Who helps you?” (subject) versus “Who do you help?” (object).
