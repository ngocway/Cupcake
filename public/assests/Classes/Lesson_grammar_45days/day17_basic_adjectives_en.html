📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. Adjective before a noun

Use an adjective before a noun to describe which kind of person or thing it is.

bag → a heavy bag
Nina carries a heavy bag.
💡 Heavy modifies bag and comes directly before that noun.
flower → a beautiful flower
There is a beautiful flower in the garden.
💡 Beautiful describes flower before the noun.

2. Adjective after be

Adjectives can follow am/is/are to describe the subject.

the room + clean → is clean
The classroom is clean.
💡 Clean describes the classroom after the linking verb is.
the children + happy → are happy
The children are happy.
💡 Happy is a description after are, not an action verb.

3. Adjectives after look, feel, seem, sound

Linking verbs connect a subject with an adjective, rather than asking how an action is done.

look + tired → looks tired
Dad looks tired after work.
💡 Looks means appears, so tired describes Dad.
sound + wonderful → sounds wonderful
Your idea sounds wonderful.
💡 Sounds links idea to its quality; an adjective is required.

4. Adjectives do not take plural -s

English adjectives keep the same form before singular and plural nouns.

one small cat → two small cats
I see two small cats.
💡 Small does not become smalls just because cats is plural.
a red apple → three red apples
We bought three red apples.
💡 Red stays unchanged; only the noun apple takes plural -s.

5. More than one adjective: opinion before size and color

When using several adjectives, a natural beginner pattern is opinion → size → color → noun.

nice + small + bag → a nice small bag
She has a nice small bag.
💡 Nice gives an opinion; small gives size, so nice normally comes first.
beautiful + blue + dress → a beautiful blue dress
My sister wears a beautiful blue dress.
💡 Opinion beautiful naturally precedes the color blue.

6. Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs often describe actions

Use adjective with a noun or linking verb; manner adverbs typically modify action verbs.

quiet child → quiet
The quiet child reads a book.
💡 Quiet describes a child, not the way the child reads.
quietly reads → quietly
The child reads quietly.
💡 Quietly describes how the reading happens, so it is an adverb.

🎯 2. Real-World Usage Scenarios

Describing a classroom

Our classroom is bright and clean.
There is a large window near the door.

At the pet shop

That small brown puppy looks friendly.
The kittens are playful.

Choosing a school bag

I like the light blue bag.
This bag feels comfortable.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She has a reds bag.
✅ She has a red bag.
💡 An adjective does not receive plural -s.
MISTAKE 2
❌ The flowers are beautifully.
✅ The flowers are beautiful.
💡 After are, use an adjective describing the flowers.
MISTAKE 3
❌ It is a bag heavy.
✅ It is a heavy bag.
💡 Attributive heavy normally goes before bag.
MISTAKE 4
❌ The soup tastes deliciously.
✅ The soup tastes delicious.
💡 Tastes is a linking verb here; delicious describes the soup.

❓ Quick FAQ

Can an adjective appear after a noun?

Yes in some special structures, but beginners most often use it before a noun or after a linking verb.

Do adjectives change for plural nouns?

No: one green apple, three green apples.

Is “feel happy” correct?

Yes. Feel acts as a linking verb and takes the adjective happy.
