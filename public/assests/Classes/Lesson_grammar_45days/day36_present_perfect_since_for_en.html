📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

Have/has + past participle

Use have for I/you/we/they and has for he/she/it.

finish → have finished
We have finished our homework.
💡 We takes have and finish has regular V3 finished.
eat → has eaten
She has eaten breakfast.
💡 She takes has and eat uses irregular V3 eaten.

Since + starting point

Since introduces when a continuing situation began.

2020 → since 2020
I have lived here since 2020.
💡 2020 is one starting point, not a duration.
Monday → since Monday
He has been ill since Monday.
💡 Monday marks the beginning of the illness.

For + duration

For expresses how long a situation has lasted.

three years → for three years
They have studied English for three years.
💡 Three years is a length of time, so use for.
two hours → for two hours
We have waited for two hours.
💡 Two hours measures duration rather than a starting point.

Regular and irregular V3

Regular participles use -ed; irregular V3 must be learned.

visit → visited
I have visited that museum.
💡 Visit uses regular V3 visited.
go → gone
She has gone to the library.
💡 Go uses V3 gone, not past V2 went.

Negatives and questions

Add not after have/has; move have/has before the subject in questions.

have finished → have not finished
We have not finished yet.
💡 Not follows have; finished stays V3.
has she seen → Has she seen?
Has she seen the film?
💡 Has goes before she while seen remains V3.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She have eaten lunch.
✅ She has eaten lunch.
💡 We takes have and finish has regular V3 finished.
MISTAKE 2
❌ I have lived here for 2020.
✅ I have lived here since 2020.
💡 2020 is one starting point, not a duration.
MISTAKE 3
❌ We have waited since two hours.
✅ We have waited for two hours.
💡 Three years is a length of time, so use for.
MISTAKE 4
❌ He has went home.
✅ He has gone home.
💡 Visit uses regular V3 visited.
