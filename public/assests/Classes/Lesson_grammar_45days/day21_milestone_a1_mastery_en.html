📖 1. Detailed Rules & Spelling Patterns

1. Choose simple or continuous from meaning

A1 checkpoint: distinguish repeated habits from actions occurring now.

every day → walks
Leo walks to school every day.
💡 Every day identifies a routine, so simple present is appropriate.
right now → is walking
Leo is walking to school right now.
💡 Right now identifies an ongoing action, so use is + -ing.

2. Apply -s / -es and do / does

Third-person singular affirmative takes -s/-es; after does in questions or negatives, use the base verb.

study + she → studies
She studies English every day.
💡 Study ends consonant + y, so y changes to ies for she.
Does she + study → Does she study?
Does she study every day?
💡 Does already marks the third-person singular, so study remains base form.

3. Choose a/an/the or zero article

Use a/an for one non-specific singular countable noun, the for identified referents, and zero article for many general plurals.

an + apple → an apple
I eat an apple after lunch.
💡 Apple begins with a vowel sound and is a singular countable noun.
general cats → cats
Cats like warm places.
💡 Cats is a plural noun used generally, so no article is required.
identified book → the book
Please open the book on your desk.
💡 The book is identified by the phrase on your desk.

4. Select some / any

Some is common in affirmative statements and offers; any is common in negatives and neutral questions.

positive → some apples
We have some apples.
💡 Some introduces an unspecified positive quantity.
negative → any apples
We do not have any apples.
💡 Any combines with negation to say no apples are available.

5. Select much / many

Many goes with countable plurals; much goes with uncountable nouns.

books → many books
How many books do you have?
💡 Books can be counted individually, so many is correct.
water → much water
How much water do you need?
💡 Water is uncountable in this sense, so use much.

6. Choose adjective or manner adverb

An adjective describes a noun/subject; an adverb describes how an action happens.

quiet student → quiet
The quiet student is reading.
💡 Quiet modifies student, a noun, so it is an adjective.
reads quietly → quietly
The student reads quietly.
💡 Quietly modifies reads and answers how the student reads.

7. Form present-time WH-questions

Choose the question word and auxiliary from the information requested and the verb pattern.

place → Where do you live?
Where do you live?
💡 Where asks for a location; do supports lexical live with you.
activity now → What are you doing?
What are you doing now?
💡 An ongoing action uses are + -ing rather than do + base form.

8. Choose in / on / at for time and place

In marks broad periods or interiors, on marks days or surfaces, and at marks precise times or points.

Monday → on Monday
Our test is on Monday.
💡 Monday is a specific weekday, so it takes on.
8:00 → at 8:00
The test starts at 8:00.
💡 An exact clock time takes at.
room → in the room
The students are in the room.
💡 The room is an enclosed place, so use in.

⚠️ 3. Common Mistakes: Wrong vs Right

MISTAKE 1
❌ She study English every day.
✅ She studies English every day.
💡 She needs third-person -s; study ends consonant + y → studies.
MISTAKE 2
❌ He is know the answer.
✅ He knows the answer.
💡 Know is normally stative and takes present simple.
MISTAKE 3
❌ How many rice do we need?
✅ How much rice do we need?
💡 Rice is uncountable, so use much.
MISTAKE 4
❌ We meet in Friday at 8.
✅ We meet on Friday at 8.
💡 Days use on; exact times use at.

❓ Quick FAQ

How should I use this checkpoint?

Read each rule, cover the explanations, then explain why each example uses its form. Review any rule you cannot justify.

What should I master for A1?

Recognize everyday routines, current actions, basic questions, descriptions, quantities and common time/place expressions.
