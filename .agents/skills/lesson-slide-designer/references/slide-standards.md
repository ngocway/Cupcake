# Slide standards for teaching

Sources: the instructional design module "Developing digital learning materials: text and images" (Institute of Technical Education, HCMC University of Technology and Education, Vietnam), Richard Mayer's principles of multimedia learning, and Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) on video engagement in MOOCs. The rules were then tested on real course decks and video lessons.

## 1. What a text-and-image learning material is

A digital learning material whose media are **text** and **images** (photos, diagrams, flowcharts, charts), carrying teaching content converted from traditional form into a form machines can display. Learners interact with it.

It comes in three forms: (1) **a slide deck that structures a chapter or lesson**; (2) a digitised book; (3) digitised teaching aids such as notes and worksheets. This skill makes form (1).

Three kinds of interaction belong in every deck:
- **Hyperlinks** to documents, videos and pages (put them in speaker notes or on the closing slide).
- **Navigation**: the agenda slide reappears before each part with the current part highlighted, so learners always know where they are.
- **Acting on content**: questions, fill-ins, pause tasks (`question` slides).

## 2. The ten design rules and how the script checks them

| # | Rule | How it is enforced |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One topic per slide** | one `title` per slide; more than one idea means a new slide |
| 2 | **Two media for one piece of content**: text plus an image, icon, diagram, number or chart | every slide type pairs text with a visual element |
| 3 | **Consistent media across the deck**: title position, labels, footer, colours, fonts | one shared frame; one theme for the whole course |
| 4 | **6x6 or 8x8 text rule**: at most 6 lines, about 6 to 8 words per line | error above 6 lines, warning on long lines |
| 5 | **Minimum projected font size: 24 pt** | everything 24 pt or more; text that does not fit stops the build, it is never shrunk |
| 6 | **Dark background, light text** to reduce eye strain | default theme `navy`; `light` only for print or when asked |
| 7 | **Sans serif type** | Arial throughout (wide language coverage, on every machine) |
| 8 | **No more than three colours** | background, text, accent; cards and muted text are tints of those |
| 9 | **No meaningless animation** | the .pptx carries no animation; reveals are written in `notes` for the video step and follow the voice |
| 10 | **One slide master**: theme, layout and text styles from one place | every slide is generated by the same code; nothing is styled by hand |

Two rules added from practice:
- **Titles, course name and lesson name in capitals** (switch off with `"title_case": "as-written"`). The JSON keeps normal case so narration and subtitles stay natural. Cards, bullets, questions and key messages keep normal case.
- **24 pt is the floor for every piece of text**, including labels, captions, sources, the footer and the slide number. Titles start at 36 pt (down to 28 when long). Safe area 0.6 inch from every edge. Three or four main blocks per slide at most.

## 3. Deck structure for one lesson

| Step | Slide | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the topic | `title` | organisation or course, lesson code, lesson title, instructor, contact, one visual |
| 2 | Introduce the objectives | `objectives` | 3 or 4 objectives with action verbs, taken from the lesson's objectives |
| 3 | Introduce the content | `agenda` | numbered parts; **shown again before each part** with `current` highlighting it |
| 4 | Develop the content | `section`, `cards`, `two-column`, `process`, `compare`, `big-numbers`, `chart`, `image`, `table`, `key-message`, `question` | one idea per slide; vary the types; at least one `question` per part |
| 5 | Review, check, close | `question`, `summary`, `closing` | end with a concrete action, never "thanks for watching" |

For video courses add a try-it-first `question` right after the title slide, and treat each video of 10 to 12 minutes or less as its own cluster with an open, a body and a close.

## 4. Multimedia learning principles behind the rules

- **Coherence**: cut words, pictures and music that do not serve the objective.
- **Redundancy**: the narration never reads the slide word for word.
- **Contiguity**: text sits next to the picture it explains.
- **Signalling**: point at exactly what you are talking about (write it in `notes`).
- **Personalisation**: a conversational voice, "you", short sentences.
- **Short videos hold attention best** (Guo, Kim and Rubin found engagement drops after about 6 minutes); content appears in step with the voice; no still frame longer than about 25 seconds.
- **Three layouts, one per slide**: centred (title, section, key message, closing), half and half (compare, one big visual with text), grid (3 to 6 similar items). Big numbers like "01." with the last digit in the accent colour; a small accent label above the title.
- **Images**: rectangles or cut-out PNGs; a real screenshot is the main visual when teaching software; no AI images containing text; check the licence of every free image and record its source.
- **One concept, one colour and one icon** for the whole course, so learners recognise it at a glance.
- **One focal point per slide**; the accent colour carries meaning (current part, correct answer, key number).

## 5. Checklist before delivery

1. All five structure steps are there (title, objectives, agenda, content with the agenda repeated, review and closing).
2. Every slide: one idea, at most 6 lines, text plus a visual, one layout.
3. `build_slides.py` finishes with **0 errors**; every warning was read and fixed or explained.
4. You looked at the contact sheet and at every slide at 1920x1080: no clipped text, no overlaps, no large empty areas, readable at a third of the size.
5. No narration reads its slide word for word; every part has a question or pause; the closing gives one action.
6. Terms match the lesson source; no number on a slide that is not in the source.
